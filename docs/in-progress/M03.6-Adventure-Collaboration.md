## Overview and objectives

M5 made every personal or side adventure a one-person thread. M6 lets a player bring other characters into one, with everyone already in it agreeing first, and lets the people in a thread decide together to show it to the party.

M6 delivers:

1. **Invitations by consent.** An accepted participant proposes another player's character. Every accepted participant approves before the invitation reaches its owner. The invitee sees only the title, type, inviter, and invited character until they accept.
2. **Leaving** an adventure, for any accepted participant but the last.
3. **Sharing with the party.** Each accepted participant gives or withholds consent. The adventure becomes party-visible only when all of them consent, and goes back to participants-only the moment anyone withdraws or a new participant joins without consenting.
4. **A party audience.** `private.current_party_campaign_ids()` (accounts that own an active, in-party character, M03 approved decision 2) reads party-shared adventures, read-only.
5. **Departure semantics for shared threads**, closing M03.1 Q4 and the M6 clauses of M03.1 §6.2.
6. **Derived state kept by the database.** Promotion from proposed to invited and the visibility of each adventure are recomputed by one function inside every write path, and a deferred constraint refuses any commit that leaves an adventure shared without full consent.
7. **Board changes at the Map table**: a "Waiting for you" group, a Participants section, sharing controls, a read-only view for the party, and a request count on the Places entry.
8. **Tests**: multi-account integration tests (three or four players per scenario) against the local stack, unit tests, and a mocked browser spec.

## Approved decisions

Approved 2026-11-16: the recommendation in each row. The alternatives are kept for history.

| # | Question | Recommendation | Alternative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | **Characters per player in one adventure** | **At most one live participation per player** (proposed, invited, or accepted) per adventure, by a partial unique index. One player is one approver and one consent; inviting a second character of someone already in it is refused. | Allow several characters per player and count approvals and consent per account. Every rule then needs a "distinct owner" step and the UI has to explain why one tick covers two rows. |
| D2 | **Who can be invited** | An **active** character of **another current member**, in the same campaign, whether or not it is in the party. Retired and deceased characters cannot be invited. Main adventures have no invitations (M03.5 D1). | Only in-party characters, or any status. |
| D3 | **Approval before an invitation is sent** | The proposer's approval is recorded with the proposal. The row becomes `invited` when **every current accepted participant** has approved. A sole participant's proposal is therefore sent at once. A participant who joins while a proposal is pending must approve it too; a participant who leaves no longer counts. | Let the proposer send directly and let the others veto afterwards. That reveals the thread's title to the invitee before everyone agrees, which PRODUCT §7.1.3 rules out. |
| D4 | **Rejecting and withdrawing** | **Any accepted participant** can cancel a proposed or invited row ("Reject" on a proposal waiting for them, "Withdraw" on a sent invitation). The row is **deleted**. A cancelled invitation simply disappears from the invitee's list; a rejected proposal was never visible to them. | Keep cancelled rows as history. That needs a sixth status and a rule for who sees it. |
| D5 | **What the invitee sees and answers** | One RPC returns, for the caller's own invited characters in a campaign: the adventure's title and type, the invited character, who invited them, when, and whether it is **currently shared with the party** (so accepting without consent is an informed choice, D6). Nothing else: no description, next step, participants, objectives, or updates. **Accept** makes the row `accepted`; **Decline** makes it `declined`, which accepted participants see as "Declined" and can re-propose through a new approval round. | Hide the shared flag, and revert sharing on every acceptance. |
| D6 | **Sharing with the party** | Consent is a flag on each accepted participant's row. "Share with party" sets your own; others see "{name} wants to share this with the party" with **Agree** and **Not now**. The adventure is party-visible exactly when **every accepted participant consents**; for a sole participant that is immediate, after a confirmation. **Not now** and **Stop sharing** clear **every** consent, so a new request starts from nothing. A player accepting an invitation to a shared adventure chooses "Keep it shared" (consent) or not; not consenting makes it private and clears every consent. | Keep other participants' consent when sharing ends, so a later consent republishes without asking them again. |
| D7 | **What the party sees** | **Read-only**: title, type, status, next step, reference, description, objectives with tick attribution, updates, and the accepted participants' characters. No edits, status changes, ticks, or posts. **Proposed, invited, declined, and left rows and approvals are hidden** from the party, so it never learns who is being invited. | Let party members tick objectives or post updates. That makes them collaborators without the consent model. |
| D8 | **Leaving an adventure** | Any accepted participant **except the last** can leave. Their row becomes `left` (shown to the remaining participants as "Left"), their consent and approvals are removed, and they lose access at once unless it is still shared with a party they belong to. Coming back needs a new invitation. The last participant is told to archive it instead (`adventure_last_participant`). | Let the last participant leave and delete the adventure (M03.5 D5). That is a destructive delete without the creator-only confirmation of PRODUCT §7.4. |
| D9 | **Leaving or removal from the campaign** (closes M03.1 Q4 and §6.2) | In the departure hook: (a) in a thread with another accepted participant, the departing player's participation becomes `left`; (b) a thread where they are the **sole** accepted participant is **kept, as M03.5 D6**, but its party share ends and its pending proposals and invitations are deleted, so no one can join a thread whose only member is away; (c) proposals and invitations **to** the departing player's characters are deleted; (d) their approvals are deleted; (e) every affected adventure is reconciled. On rejoining, `left` rows stay `left`; sole threads return unchanged and private. | Mark every participation `left`, including sole ones. That loses solo threads for good on a misclick, which M03.5 D6 avoided. |
| D10 | **Archive with several participants** (changes M03.5 D4 for personal and side) | The creator archives and restores while an accepted participant. Once the creator has left, **any accepted participant** may. An archived personal or side adventure stays **readable, read-only, by its accepted participants** (in their Archived group), so archiving never hides others' contributions from them; it leaves the party view at once. Pending proposals and invitations are frozen while it is archived (hidden from the invitee, unanswerable). Main adventures are unchanged: creator only, hidden from everyone else. | Keep "creator only, hidden from everyone but the creator". With collaborators, that lets one player hide a shared thread from the others. |
| D11 | **A participating character is deleted** | Its row cascades. If accepted participants remain, the adventure stays and is reconciled (pending proposals may be sent; sharing may complete). If none remain, M03.5 D5 deletes it. The tent's confirmation gives both counts: "It will leave 2 shared adventures. 1 adventure only it is in will be deleted." | One count, as today. |
| D12 | **Limits** | **10 live participations** (proposed, invited, or accepted) per adventure, matching the camp's ten seats. Re-proposing a declined or left character is allowed, through a full approval round each time. | A cooldown before a declined character can be proposed again. |
| D13 | **Discoverability without Realtime** | A **"Waiting for you"** group at the top of the board lists invitations to you, proposals awaiting your approval, and share requests awaiting your consent. One RPC, `count_my_adventure_requests`, is read when the camp opens and on focus, and the Places entry shows "Adventures (2)" while it is above zero. No scene-art change and no notifications. | No count: players find requests only by opening the board. |
| D14 | **Concurrency for collaboration actions** | Every collaboration RPC locks campaign → character (where one is involved) → adventure, re-checks state under the lock, and returns a specific stale-state code (`adventure_invitation_unavailable`, `adventure_proposal_unavailable`) that the UI turns into "This changed. The adventure has been refreshed." No `updated_at` token is needed. | Optimistic tokens on participant rows. |
| D15 | **Out of scope** | Removing another participant (needs their agreement, PRODUCT §7.1.5); transferring the creator role; edit or audit history; Realtime (M7); DM handoffs (M8); an Overview "next steps" card; one inbox for invitations across campaigns; email or push notifications. | Plan any of these as an M6 extension. |

## 2. Database schema, constraints, and relationships

### 2.1 Table changes

```sql
alter table public.adventures drop constraint adventures_visibility_check;
alter table public.adventures add constraint adventures_visibility_check
  check (visibility in ('participants', 'party', 'campaign'));

alter table public.adventure_participants
  add column invited_by uuid references public.profiles (id) on delete set null,
  add column share_consent boolean not null default false,
  add column share_consent_at timestamptz,
  add column responded_at timestamptz,
  add column updated_at timestamptz not null default now(),
  add constraint adventure_participants_consent_check
    check ((not share_consent or status = 'accepted') and (share_consent = (share_consent_at is not null)));

-- D1: one live participation per player per adventure. M5 rows are one accepted row each.
create unique index adventure_participants_one_live_per_player_idx
  on public.adventure_participants (adventure_id, profile_id)
  where status in ('proposed', 'invited', 'accepted');

create table public.adventure_invite_approvals (
  participant_id uuid not null references public.adventure_participants (id) on delete cascade,
  approver_id uuid not null references public.profiles (id) on delete cascade,
  approved_at timestamptz not null default now(),
  primary key (participant_id, approver_id)
);

create index adventure_invite_approvals_approver_idx on public.adventure_invite_approvals (approver_id);
```

- Only approvals are stored. Rejection deletes the proposal (D4), which cascades its approvals.
- Existing M5 rows get `share_consent = false`, `invited_by = null`, `responded_at = null`, and `updated_at = now()`. No other backfill. The partial unique index cannot fail on M5 data, because M5 writes one participant per adventure; Phase 0 confirms it with a read-only query.
- `adventure_participants_unique (adventure_id, character_id)` stays. A declined or left character is re-proposed by resetting its row (§2.6), not by inserting a second one.

### 2.2 Party and participation helpers

```sql
-- M03 approved decision 2: campaigns where the caller is a current member and owns an
-- active, in-party character (in_party already requires status = 'active').
create function private.current_party_campaign_ids()
returns setof uuid
language sql stable security definer set search_path = ''
as $$
  select distinct c.campaign_id
  from public.characters as c
  where c.owner_id = (select auth.uid())
    and c.in_party
    and c.campaign_id in (select private.current_campaign_ids());
$$;

-- Adventures where the caller owns an accepted participant, archived ones included.
create function private.participating_adventure_ids()
returns setof uuid
language sql stable security definer set search_path = ''
as $$
  select p.adventure_id
  from public.adventure_participants as p
  where p.profile_id = (select auth.uid())
    and p.status = 'accepted'
    and p.campaign_id in (select private.current_campaign_ids());
$$;
```

### 2.3 Visibility helpers (replaced)

```sql
-- Readable: main (archived only for its creator), the caller's own threads (archived included,
-- D10), and live party-shared threads for the party (D7).
create or replace function private.visible_adventure_ids()
returns setof uuid
language sql stable security definer set search_path = ''
as $$
  select a.id
  from public.adventures as a
  where a.campaign_id in (select private.current_campaign_ids())
    and (
      (a.type = 'main' and (a.archived_at is null or a.created_by = (select auth.uid())))
      or a.id in (select private.participating_adventure_ids())
      or (a.visibility = 'party' and a.archived_at is null
          and a.campaign_id in (select private.current_party_campaign_ids()))
    );
$$;

-- Updatable rows (the adventures update policy): the visible set without the party audience.
create function private.writable_adventure_ids()
returns setof uuid
language sql stable security definer set search_path = ''
as $$
  select a.id
  from public.adventures as a
  where a.campaign_id in (select private.current_campaign_ids())
    and (
      (a.type = 'main' and (a.archived_at is null or a.created_by = (select auth.uid())))
      or a.id in (select private.participating_adventure_ids())
    );
$$;

-- Where objectives and updates may be written: writable and not archived.
create or replace function private.editable_adventure_ids()
returns setof uuid
language sql stable security definer set search_path = ''
as $$
  select w.id from private.writable_adventure_ids() as w(id)
  join public.adventures as a on a.id = w.id
  where a.archived_at is null;
$$;
```

All three are executable by `authenticated` and `postgres` only. A party-shared adventure becomes invisible to the party the moment any of these change: the share ends, the viewer's character leaves the party or retires, or the viewer leaves the campaign.

### 2.4 Derived state: `private.reconcile_adventure(p_adventure_id uuid)`

Called by every collaboration RPC, the participant after-delete trigger, and the departure hook, always with the adventure row already locked. Not executable by clients.

1. **Promote proposals (D3).** Every `proposed` row whose approvals cover every accepted participant's `profile_id` becomes `invited`. Accepted participants of departed members never exist alongside proposals (D9 b), so "accepted" here means current members.
2. **Visibility (D6).** For personal and side: `party` when there is at least one accepted participant, every one has consented, and the adventure is not archived; otherwise `participants`. Main is untouched.
3. **Clearing consent.** If step 2 moved the adventure from `party` to `participants`, every consent is cleared in the same call (D6: a new share starts from nothing). When the cause was archiving, consent is cleared as well, so restoring an adventure never republishes it silently.

`private.derived_adventure_visibility(p_adventure_id uuid)` holds step 2's rule so the guard and the constraint (§2.5) use the same definition.

### 2.5 Guards and the consent constraint

**`private.guard_adventure_write`** (replaced; before update on `adventures`). The M5 rules stay, with three changes:

- `visibility` may change only to `private.derived_adventure_visibility(new.id)` evaluated with the new row's archive state; anything else → `adventure_visibility_forbidden` (`23000`). Clients have no grant on the column, so in practice only `reconcile_adventure` changes it.
- Archive and restore of personal and side follow D10: the creator while an accepted participant, or any accepted participant once the creator is not one → otherwise `adventure_archive_creator_only` (`42501`). Main is unchanged.
- Archiving calls `reconcile_adventure` through an after-update trigger, so pending proposals freeze and the share ends (§2.4 step 3).

**`private.guard_adventure_participant_write`** (replaced; before insert or update on `adventure_participants`):

| Rule | Error |
| --- | --- |
| Keys never change (`id`, `adventure_id`, `campaign_id`, `character_id`, `profile_id`, `created_at`) | `adventure_reassignment_forbidden` (`23000`) |
| No participants on a main adventure | `adventure_main_has_no_participants` |
| Insert as `accepted` only from `create_adventure` (the adventure has no participant yet) or as `proposed` | `adventure_participant_transition_forbidden` |
| Allowed transitions: `proposed → invited`, `invited → accepted`, `invited → declined`, `accepted → left`, `declined → proposed`, `left → proposed` | `adventure_participant_transition_forbidden` |
| Consent only on `accepted` rows; leaving clears it | the check in §2.1 |
| A transition sets `updated_at`, and `responded_at` on accept or decline; re-proposal clears `responded_at` and consent and sets `invited_by` | — |

Nested updates (`pg_trigger_depth() > 1`, the `invited_by` `set null` action) pass through, as every guard does.

**`private.check_adventure_share_consent`** — a `constraint trigger … deferrable initially deferred` on `adventure_participants` (insert, update, delete) and on `adventures` (update of `visibility`, `archived_at`). At commit it raises `adventure_share_consent_missing` (`23000`) if the adventure is `party` and is archived, has no accepted participant, or has an accepted participant without consent. This is the privacy invariant, checked for every write path, including `postgres` and service-role tooling. The reverse (everyone consents but it is not shared) is not a leak and is left to `reconcile_adventure`.

**`private.after_adventure_participant_delete`** (replaces `delete_orphaned_adventure`; after delete, for each row): deletes a non-main adventure with no accepted participant left (M03.5 D5); otherwise reconciles it (D11). When the adventure itself is being deleted, it is already gone and the trigger does nothing.

**Cap** (inside `propose_adventure_participant`, under the adventure lock): 10 live rows → `adventure_participant_limit_reached` (D12).

All guard and trigger functions are executable by nobody but `postgres`.

### 2.6 RPCs

All `security definer set search_path = ''`, executable by `authenticated` only. "Accepted participant" always means the caller owns an accepted row in the adventure and is a current member. Lock order campaign `FOR SHARE` → character `FOR SHARE` → adventure `FOR UPDATE` (D14).

| RPC | Who | Behaviour | Errors |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `propose_adventure_participant(p_adventure_id, p_character_id) returns uuid` | Accepted participant | Personal or side, not archived. The character is active, in the campaign, owned by another current member (D2), and that player has no live row here (D1). Inserts a `proposed` row, or resets the character's `declined` or `left` row; sets `invited_by`; records the caller's approval; reconciles (a sole participant's proposal becomes `invited` at once). Returns the participant id. | `adventure_unavailable`, `adventure_archived`, `adventure_main_has_no_participants`, `adventure_character_unavailable`, `adventure_player_already_participating`, `adventure_participant_limit_reached` |
| `approve_adventure_participant(p_participant_id)` | Accepted participant who has not approved | Row is `proposed`, adventure not archived. Records the approval; reconciles. | `adventure_proposal_unavailable` |

`invited_at` is the row's `updated_at` at the moment it became `invited`.

### 2.7 Departure hook (replaced)

`private.handle_campaign_member_departure(p_campaign_id, p_profile_id)`, still called by `leave_campaign` and `remove_campaign_member` under their campaign lock:

1. Clear `in_party` on the player's characters (unchanged).
2. Delete `proposed` and `invited` rows whose `profile_id` is the departing player (D9 c).
3. Delete the departing player's approvals in the campaign (D9 d).
4. For each adventure where they own an accepted row, locked in id order:
   - another accepted participant exists → their row becomes `left` with consent cleared (D9 a);
   - they are the sole accepted participant → clear their consent and delete the adventure's `proposed` and `invited` rows (D9 b).
5. Reconcile every adventure touched in steps 2–4.

The comment above the function is rewritten to describe this, and M03.1 §6.2 and Q4 are updated to point here.

### 2.8 Relationships and delete behaviour

```text
adventures 1 ──< adventure_participants >── 1 characters (same campaign, same owner)
                         │   └── invited_by → profiles (set null)
                         └──< adventure_invite_approvals >── 1 profiles (approver)
```

| Event | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Participant leaves the adventure | Row `left`, consent and approvals removed, reconciled. |
| Participating character deleted | Row cascades; adventure deleted if no accepted participant remains, otherwise reconciled (D11). |
| Character retired, marked deceased, or taken out of the party | Participation unchanged. Its owner stops seeing party-shared threads they are not in. |
| Player leaves or is removed from the campaign | D9. |
| Player rejoins | Sole threads return, private. `left` rows stay `left`. |
| Player's account deleted | Their characters cascade (as above), their approvals cascade, `invited_by` becomes null. Never blocked by a guard. |
| Adventure archived | Share ends, consent cleared, proposals and invitations frozen (D10). Restore brings them back; sharing needs new consent. |
| Campaign deleted | Everything cascades. |
| Ownership transfer | No effect. |

### 3.1 Grants

```sql

grant select on table public.adventure_invite_approvals to authenticated;
-- adventure_participants: still select only. Every write is an RPC.
```

### 3.2 Policies

```sql
-- adventures: select unchanged in text (the helper changed).
drop policy "Readers edit visible adventures" on public.adventures;
create policy "Writers edit their adventures" on public.adventures
  for update to authenticated
  using (id in (select private.writable_adventure_ids()))
  with check (id in (select private.writable_adventure_ids()));

drop policy "Readers see participants" on public.adventure_participants;
create policy "Readers see accepted participants; participants see the rest"
  on public.adventure_participants for select to authenticated
  using (
    (status = 'accepted' and adventure_id in (select private.visible_adventure_ids()))
    or adventure_id in (select private.participating_adventure_ids())
  );

create policy "Participants see approvals" on public.adventure_invite_approvals
  for select to authenticated
  using (exists (
    select 1 from public.adventure_participants as p
    where p.id = participant_id
      and p.adventure_id in (select private.participating_adventure_ids())
  ));
```

Objective and update policies keep their text; `editable_adventure_ids()` now excludes the party, so the party can read but not write them.

**Explicit negatives to test:** an invited player cannot read the description, next step, objectives, updates, or participant list, through any table, embed, or count, before accepting. A proposed player sees nothing at all, including through `list_my_adventure_invitations` and `count_my_adventure_requests`. ## 4. Workflows and requests

### 4.1 Requests

| Action | Request | Error handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| List | M5 `listSelect` with `participants:adventure_participants(id,character_id,profile_id,status,share_consent,approvals:adventure_invite_approvals(approver_id))` | Unchanged. |

| Character delete counts | The character's accepted rows, then the accepted rows of those adventures (both readable by the character's owner); `sole` and `shared` computed in the client | Error → the sentence is left out, as in M5. |

### 4.2 Concurrency

| Race | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Two participants approve the last two approvals at once | The adventure lock serializes them; the second approval promotes the row. Exactly one `invited` row. |
| A participant rejects while another approves | Whichever is first wins; the other gets `adventure_proposal_unavailable`. |
| The invitee accepts while a participant withdraws | Serialized: either the withdrawal fails with `adventure_proposal_unavailable` or the acceptance fails with `adventure_invitation_unavailable`. |
| The last consent and a withdrawal at once | Serialized: either it shares and then ends, clearing consent, or the withdrawal comes first and the consent leaves a fresh request. Never shared with a missing consent (the deferred check). |
| Two participants leave at once, leaving one | Serialized; the second sees whether it is now last and gets `adventure_last_participant` if so. |
| A proposal while the invitee's character is deleted | The character lock serializes them: either `adventure_character_unavailable`, or the new row cascades away. |
| An acceptance while the invitee is removed from the campaign | The campaign lock serializes them: either the acceptance fails, or it succeeds and the hook then marks the row `left` (D9). |
| Parallel proposals at 9 live rows | Exactly one succeeds. |

Invariants after any interleaving (asserted by tests): no adventure is `party` with an accepted participant lacking consent, archived, or without accepted participants; no `proposed` row has approvals from every accepted participant; at most one live row per player per adventure; no accepted participant of a departed member shares an adventure with another accepted participant or with live pending rows; and the M03.5 §4.2 invariants still hold.

## 8. Non-destructive migration strategy

### 8.1 Files (timestamps after `20261116160128`; use the real creation time)

Wrapped in `begin; … commit;`. It changes only the M5 adventure objects listed in §1.1 and the departure hook. Existing rows keep their values; the new columns take their defaults.

### 8.2 Pre-flight (read-only)

1. `git status` is clean, or contains M6 changes only.
2. `npm run supabase:status`. `schema_migrations` lists exactly the 25 versions ending `20261116160128`.
3. Baseline in a read-only transaction: row counts of every `public` table; every adventure's visibility matches its type; no adventure has more than one participant row (so the partial unique index is safe); the ownership-consistency query (0 rows).
4. Optional local safety net outside the repo: `pg_dump --data-only` of `public` and `auth` to the scratchpad.
5. Baseline suites: `npm run lint`, `npm run format:check`, `npm run build`, `npm test`, `npm run test:local-supabase`. `test:e2e` is not run (your preference).

### 8.3 Apply

`npm run db:migrate` (from `implementation/backend`), then `npm run db:types` and `npx prettier --write src/types/database.ts`. Never `db:reset`.

### 8.4 Post-migration checks (read-only)

| Check | Expected |
| --- | --- |
| Existing row counts | Identical to baseline (profiles may rise only from test accounts) |
| Existing adventures | Same visibility, participants all `accepted` with `share_consent = false` |
| `relrowsecurity` on `adventure_invite_approvals` | true |
| Table and column privileges | M5's, plus select on approvals for `authenticated`. No write grant on participants or approvals. |

### 8.5 Rollback (compensating migration only)

Restore the M5 versions of the replaced functions, triggers, and policies; set every personal and side adventure back to `participants`; delete every non-accepted participant row; drop the approvals table, the partial index, and the new columns. M5's helpers read only accepted rows, so `left` rows simply stop granting access once deleted. **This loses invitations, consents, and departure history, and leaves multi-participant threads that M5's UI never expected.** Export first; acceptable only before real use.

### 8.6 Test-data safety

As M5: tests create their own accounts and campaigns and delete them in `finally`; counts are scoped to the test's campaigns; direct `postgres` writes run inside `begin; … rollback;` through `localDatabaseQuery`.

## 9. Testing

### 9.1 Integration — `e2e/local-supabase-adventure-collaboration.spec.ts` (new, added to `test:local-supabase`)

Scenarios use an owner and three players (A, B, C), each with an active character; C's character starts outside the party where noted.

- **Catalog and privileges**: exactly §8.4.
- **Proposal and approval**: A (sole) proposes B → `invited` at once. With A and B accepted, A proposes C → `proposed`; C sees nothing through any table, embed, invitation list, or count; B approves → `invited`. A newly accepted participant blocks a pending proposal until they approve; a participant leaving unblocks it.
- **Proposal errors**: main, archived, own second character, a player already in it, a retired character, a departed member's character, another campaign's character, a random UUID, the 11th live row, and five racing proposals at 9.

- **Reject and withdraw**: either participant deletes a proposal or invitation; the invitee's list empties; answering afterwards → `adventure_invitation_unavailable`.
- **Leave**: `left`, access lost, approvals and consent removed; the last participant → `adventure_last_participant`.
- **Sharing**: A's consent alone does not share a two-person thread; B's completes it; a party member sees it read-only (every write refused or 0 rows); a member outside the party and the campaign owner outside the party see nothing; Not now and Stop sharing clear every consent; a new participant accepting without consent reverts it, with consent keeps it; a viewer whose character leaves the party or retires loses it.
- **Party audience writes**: details, status, objectives, updates, archive → 0 rows or `42501`.
- **Archive (D10)**: the creator archives; the other participant still reads it, cannot edit it; the share ends with consent cleared; pending invitations disappear from the invitee and cannot be answered; after the creator leaves, the other participant can restore.
- **Bypass through the API**: insert or update on participants and approvals → `42501`; `visibility` in an update → refused.
- **Bypass in the database** (as `postgres`, rolled back): an invalid transition, a wrong visibility, a party share with a missing consent (fails at commit), and keys moved.
- **Departure and rejoin (D9)**: in a shared thread the leaver becomes `left`; a sole thread is kept with its pending rows deleted and its share ended; invitations to the leaver are deleted; their approvals are deleted and pending proposals are promoted if that completes them; rejoining restores sole threads only. - **Character and account deletion (D11)**: a shared thread survives and is reconciled; a sole thread is deleted; account deletion cascades and nulls `invited_by` without a guard blocking it.
- **Concurrency**: every §4.2 race, then the §4.2 invariants.

## 11. Acceptance criteria (M6 as a whole)

1. An accepted participant can propose another player's active character; the invitation reaches its owner only after every accepted participant approves.
2. Before accepting, the invitee sees only the title, type, inviter, invited character, and whether it is shared; after accepting, they collaborate fully. Declining, rejecting, and withdrawing work and leave no trace for the invitee.
3. Any participant but the last can leave and loses access at once.
4. An adventure becomes party-visible only when every accepted participant consents. Any withdrawal, or a new participant who does not consent, makes it private again and clears every consent.
5. Party members (accounts with an active, in-party character) read a shared adventure but cannot change it. Other members, and the campaign owner without an in-party character, see nothing of it.
6. No list, count, embed, or RPC reveals a private thread, a pending invitation, or an approval to anyone outside its accepted participants, and no write path can leave a thread shared without full consent.
7. Leaving or removal from the campaign follows D9, and rejoining restores only sole threads.
8. Existing behaviour and tests are unchanged except where D6 and D10 change M5's rules, each recorded in the log.
9. The migration ran with `db:migrate` only, existing counts are unchanged, no `db:reset` was run, and every §9.4 command run is reported.

## 12. Risks and edge cases

### Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| A reconcile path is missed and a thread stays shared after consent is withdrawn | The deferred constraint refuses the commit on every write path; tests try each path, including `postgres`. |
| The party helper leaks a thread to someone outside the party | One helper, used only inside `visible_adventure_ids()`; tests cover non-party members, retired characters, characters taken out of the party, and departed players. |
| Pending rows reveal who is being invited | The participants policy shows non-accepted rows to accepted participants only; the invitation RPC returns the caller's own invited rows only; tests read as the party and as the proposed player. |
| Without Realtime, invitations and requests are noticed late | The Places count on camp open and focus, and the "Waiting for you" group. Realtime is M7. |
| Re-proposals pester a player who declined (D12) | Each re-proposal needs every participant's approval, and the invitee can decline again. A cooldown is D12's alternative. |
| Archiving now leaves a thread visible to its participants (D10) | Intended: it protects collaborators' contributions. It still leaves the party view and freezes invitations. |
| Changing the M5 guard and helpers regresses M5 behaviour | M5's 21 integration tests run unchanged except the assertions D6 and D10 change, each listed. |
| The departure hook does more work inside the membership transaction | It touches only the departing player's rows in one campaign, bounded by the 200-adventure and 10-participant caps. |
| Lock-order mistakes cause deadlocks | One documented order (§1.1); the §4.2 races run in tests. |

### Edge cases covered

- A sole participant can share immediately, after a confirmation.
- A participant leaving can complete a pending share request (the remaining participants have all consented) or a pending proposal (the remaining participants have all approved). Both follow "every current participant agreed"; the leave confirmation does not mention it.
- An invitee who is already a party viewer of a shared thread sees it before accepting, as any party member does; the invitation adds nothing.
- An invitation to a player whose character is then retired stays answerable; a retired character cannot be newly proposed.
- A participant who owns no in-party character still sees every thread they are in.
- Restoring an archived thread never republishes it.

- **M8**: handoff inserts by accepted participants only; a handoff never changes participants or visibility.
- **Later**: removing a participant with their agreement; transferring the creator role; edit history for shared threads.
